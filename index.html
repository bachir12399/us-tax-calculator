Frequently asked questions

On state tax for remote and B2B contract work.

Do 1099 contractors pay state tax based on where they live or where the client is located?

Generally, income tax follows where the work is physically performed and where you're a resident, not where your client is headquartered. A contractor based in Texas working remotely for a New York company typically doesn't owe New York state tax, since Texas has no income tax and the work isn't performed in New York.

What is the "convenience of the employer" rule?

A small number of states, including New York and Connecticut, tax nonresident employees on income earned while working remotely if the arrangement is for the employee's own convenience rather than a genuine business requirement. It applies mainly to payroll employees, and its reach for independent contractors is narrower and less settled.

Do I need to file a tax return in more than one state?

You may, if you're a resident of one state and perform work while physically present in another. Most states offer a credit for taxes paid to another state on the same income, so you typically aren't taxed twice on identical earnings, though you may still need to file two returns.

How much should I set aside for self-employment tax?

Self-employment tax covers Social Security and Medicare and totals 15.3% on roughly 92.35% of your net business profit, up to the Social Security wage base, plus 2.9% Medicare on everything above that. Many contractors set aside 25 to 30% of net profit to cover both self-employment and income tax.

What business expenses can reduce my taxable income?

Common deductible costs include a home office, business software and subscriptions, equipment, professional services, business travel and mileage, and health insurance premiums for the self-employed. Keeping receipts and a simple expense log throughout the year makes filing far easier.

When are quarterly estimated tax payments due?

The IRS generally expects estimated payments four times a year, due around April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the following year. Many states with an income tax run on a similar quarterly schedule.

Which states have no personal income tax?

Texas, Florida, Washington, Nevada, Wyoming, South Dakota, Alaska, and Tennessee don't tax personal income. New Hampshire only taxes interest and dividend income, not wages or business profit.
